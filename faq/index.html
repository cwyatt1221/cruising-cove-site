Frequently asked

The questions everyone actually asks.

Real, commonly-asked questions about Disney Cruise Line, answered plainly — no fluff, no guessing.

Do I need a passport for a Disney cruise?

For "closed-loop" sailings — cruises that depart from and return to the same U.S. port — U.S. citizens technically only need a certified birth certificate with a raised seal plus a government-issued photo ID (for guests 16+). A passport is still strongly recommended even when not required — it's the only document accepted if you need to fly home unexpectedly from a port, and it simplifies re-entry to the U.S. For any cruise that isn't closed-loop (international one-way sailings, repositioning cruises), a passport is required for all ages, including infants.

What's the minimum age to sail?

For most itineraries, guests must be at least 6 months old at the time of embarkation. For certain repositioning cruises (South Pacific, Transatlantic, Hawaii, and Panama Canal), the minimum rises to 1 year old. Separately, Disney will refuse passage to anyone who has entered, or will enter, their 24th week of pregnancy by the time the cruise ends.

What's the drinking age onboard?

21, on virtually every sailing — U.S. law governs the ship on any cruise departing a U.S. port, regardless of where you are at sea. The one real exception: on select cruises departing certain overseas ports (parts of Europe, and round-trip sailings from Australia, New Zealand, or Singapore), guests 18–20 may be permitted to drink, sometimes only with a signed parental consent form. If you're sailing from the U.S., plan around 21 without exception.

Can I bring my own alcohol onboard?

Yes, but the policy tightened in June 2026. Guests 21+ may now bring one unopened bottle of wine or champagne, plus six beers, packed in carry-on luggage at embarkation only. This is down from the previous allowance of two bottles, and you can no longer replenish your alcohol allotment at each port — that used to be allowed and no longer is. You can still buy alcohol at a port stop, but Disney holds it until the end of the cruise rather than letting you bring it onboard immediately.

Will I get seasick?

For most people, no. Modern cruise ships (including every DCL ship) have stabilizers that largely eliminate side-to-side rolling, and DCL's larger ships in particular are quite stable in typical Caribbean and Bahamas conditions. If you're prone to motion sickness, the ship's medical center can provide remedies, and booking a stateroom on a lower, more midship deck (see each ship's stateroom guide) minimizes motion further.

What's actually included in the cruise fare, and what isn't?

Included: your stateroom, all rotational dining, entertainment, youth clubs, and most private-island beach access including the BBQ lunch. Not included: gratuities, specialty dining (Palo, Remy, Enchanté), alcohol, Wi-Fi, spa services, and excursions. See the full cost breakdown for real numbers on each of these.

How far in advance should I book?

Earlier than most first-time cruisers expect. Popular sailings (school breaks, holiday weeks) and the best stateroom categories can sell out 9–12 months ahead, and Castaway Club returning-guest booking windows open even earlier for repeat cruisers. Booking early also means more time for the price-monitoring benefit a travel agent provides (see our Travel Agents page) to actually pay off if the fare drops before you sail.

Is Wi-Fi included?

Basic connectivity for iMessage, WhatsApp, and the Disney Cruise Line Navigator app is free. Real browsing, email, or streaming requires a paid Wi-Fi package, tiered by speed and data allowance — see the cost breakdown page for current pricing.

What if I'm a solo parent traveling with one child?

This isn't a DCL-specific restriction — a single parent or guardian can absolutely book and sail with their child, no special documentation required beyond the usual proof of citizenship for both of you. If a child is traveling with only one parent and that could raise a custody question at security or immigration in some ports, some experienced cruisers recommend carrying a simple signed permission note from the non-traveling parent, though DCL itself doesn't require it for standard sailings.
