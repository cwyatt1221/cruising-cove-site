Port of call · Mexican Riviera

Cabo San Lucas.

Land’s End Arch views, tendering into the marina, and beach or boat days — common on Disney Magic Panama Canal and Mexican Riviera-style sailings.

— The day

Pick one job for the day

Cabo works best when you choose Arch / Land’s End boat, beach club, or marina & downtown — not a stacked combo that ignores tender lines. Confirm tender operations and all-aboard in the Navigator app; rough seas can delay or cancel tenders.

Signature sight

Land’s End Arch

Glass-bottom or open boat to El Arco and Lover’s Beach viewpoints — the photo most Cabo days are built around.

Most families

Beach club day

Chairs, lunch, and a timed return. Medano-area beaches are calmer for swimming than the Pacific side near the Arch.

Low transfer

Marina & downtown

Shopping, lunch, and marina walks after you clear the tender pier — fine if you don’t need a long beach transfer.

— Disney vs independent

What to book, what to skip

  • Book Disney when: you want a guaranteed Arch boat or beach club with the ship-wait cushion, or you’re tendering with little kids and prefer one ticketed package.
  • Go independent when: marina lunch + a short Medano beach hop with a clear taxi plan and a real tender buffer.
  • Skip: Pacific-side swimming near Land’s End — currents are serious; view from a boat instead.
  • Skip: stacking Arch boat + far beach club + heavy shopping in one call.
— Transportation

How far is everything?

Destination From tender pier How most families go Notes
Marina / downtown Short walk Walk Closest option after tendering
Medano Beach clubs ~10–25 min Taxi or club shuttle Agree fare before loading; keep cash
Land’s End boat Departs marina area Disney or independent boat Build buffer for tender lines both ways
Corridor / far resorts 30–60+ min Only with a long call Easy to burn the day in traffic
  • Tenders. Cabo is often a tender port. Lines can form at peak times — leave earlier than a pier call would require.
  • Taxis. Plentiful near the marina. Settle price before you ride; round-trip + wait arrangements matter for beach clubs.
  • Disney transfer. Best for Arch boats and farther clubs when you want the miss-ship cushion. See Port Adventures filters.
— Food

What to eat ashore

  • Marina. Plenty of tourist restaurants — fine for a sit-down taco or seafood lunch if you’re not beach-bound.
  • Beach clubs. Packages that include lunch keep the day simple once you’re sandy.
  • Kids. Familiar grill options at clubs beat a long hunt for “the perfect” café when tender timing is tight.
— Shopping

What’s worth bringing home

  • Marina shops. Souvenirs, silver, and resort wear — set a budget; prices skew tourist.
  • Pressure pitches. Timeshare and “special tour” offers near the pier are common. Decline and keep moving.
— Safety

With kids in daylight

  • Swim on the Sea of Cortez / Medano side — not the Pacific open-ocean side at Land’s End.
  • Sun and dehydration are the usual risks. Hats, reef-safe sunscreen, water.
  • Stick to known clubs, marina tourist blocks, and booked boat operators.
  • Cash for taxis and tips; cards work widely at tourist spots — carry a backup.
— Timing

Work backward from all-aboard

  • Add tender wait time to every plan — both going ashore and returning.
  • If seas are rough, have a marina backup day ready in case boat tours cancel.
  • Be in the tender line with more buffer than a docked Caribbean call.
— Priority

The one thing repeats push

See El Arco from the water. Even a short boat loop beats a day that never leaves the marina shops. Pair it with lunch ashore — don’t try to do every beach club on the corridor too.
