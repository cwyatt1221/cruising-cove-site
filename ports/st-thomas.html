St. Thomas.

A U.S. territory with duty-free shopping and one of the Caribbean’s prettiest beaches a short ride away — pick beach or downtown before you step off.

— The day

Three clean options

St. Thomas splits cleanly into Magens Bay beach day, Charlotte Amalie shopping, or a snorkel / sail excursion. It’s a U.S. territory, which means duty-free shopping and no currency exchange — but it doesn’t mean the pier is downtown. Check your Navigator app for which dock you’re on.

Most families

Magens Bay

Calm, protected water and soft sand, regularly ranked among the world’s best beaches. A taxi ride from most piers, not a walk.

In the water

Snorkel / sail

Boat-based reef stops or a catamaran sail — good for families who want structured water time without renting gear themselves.

Low transfer

Charlotte Amalie shopping

Duty-free jewelry, liquor, and watches lining the historic downtown streets — walkable from most cruise piers.

How far is everything?

  • Open-air taxis. St. Thomas’s signature “safari bus” taxis run set routes to Magens Bay and back — plentiful at the pier, agree the fare first.
  • U.S. territory perks. No currency exchange, no passport hassle for U.S. citizens beyond your normal cruise documents, and generous duty-free allowances make shopping simpler here than most stops.
  • Disney transfer. Worth it for Magens Bay or a boat excursion when you want the ship-wait guarantee. See Eastern Caribbean Port Adventure filters.
— Food

What to eat ashore

  • Magens Bay. Beach-side food shacks handle burgers, grilled fish, and cold drinks — simple and reliable for a swim day.
  • Charlotte Amalie. A mix of Caribbean and American-style restaurants a few streets off the main shopping drag beat the first pier-adjacent menu.
  • Boat tours. Some sail/snorkel trips include a light lunch or rum punch — check your specific booking.
  • Kids. Familiar beach-shack menus are the easy call over hunting for something fancier on a short window.
— Shopping

What’s worth bringing home

  • Duty-free jewelry, watches, and liquor. St. Thomas is one of the Caribbean’s best-known duty-free stops — compare against home prices before assuming it’s automatically a deal.
  • Local crafts and rum. Lower-stakes souvenirs than jewelry, and easy to find in the same downtown blocks.
  • Pier stores. Convenient for forgotten sunscreen or a hat; not usually where the best jewelry prices are.
— Safety

With kids in daylight

  • Charlotte Amalie’s main shopping streets and Magens Bay are well-trafficked and generally comfortable in daylight with normal city awareness.
  • Open-air taxis carry multiple parties — hold onto kids and bags getting in and out at stops.
  • Magens Bay’s water is calm and family-friendly, but reef-safe sunscreen and shade breaks still matter — the bay gets hot with little natural shade on the sand.
  • As a U.S. territory, cards work widely; still keep some cash for taxis and beach vendors.

— Don’t miss this

If you only do one thing, most families pick Magens Bay over more shopping. The duty-free stores will be there on a future trip; a calm-water swim day with the kids usually isn’t what you regret skipping.

— Don’t miss

The one thing repeats push

Get to Magens Bay, even for a couple of hours. It’s consistently ranked among the world’s best beaches, and the protected, gently sloping water makes it genuinely easy for young kids. Charlotte Amalie’s shopping is worth a stop, but it’s the side quest here, not the main event.
