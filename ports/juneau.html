Port of call · Alaska

Juneau.

Alaska’s capital, reachable only by boat or plane — glacier views, whales, and a compact downtown are all within reach of the pier, if you dress for the weather.

— The day

Three clean options

Juneau packs a lot into one call: glacier, wildlife on the water, or downtown on foot. Unlike most Caribbean ports, the pier drops you right into the small downtown core — the harder decision is how far out of town to go for the glacier or whale-watching boats.

Signature sight

Mendenhall Glacier

A drive-up glacier with a visitor center, viewpoints, and (conditions permitting) trails to nearby falls — no hiking required to see it well.

Wildlife

Whale watching

Humpback sightings are common on Juneau boat tours in season — one of the more reliable whale-watching stops on an Alaska itinerary.

Low transfer

Downtown on foot

Compact, walkable streets right off the pier — shops, the Red Dog Saloon, and a tram to mountain views without a long transfer.

  • No rental-car necessity. Downtown, the tram, and many tour meeting points are all walkable from the pier — a real difference from most Caribbean stops.
  • Independent shuttles. Mendenhall Glacier shuttles are common and simpler than a formal excursion for families comfortable arranging their own return time.
  • Disney transfer. Worth it for glacier or whale-watching days when you want the ship-wait guarantee on a boat- or drive-based tour. See Port Adventures filters.
— Food

What to eat ashore

  • Fresh seafood. Wild Alaska salmon and halibut show up on nearly every downtown menu — a fish-and-chips stand or a sit-down spot both work for a short window.
  • Tracy’s King Crab Shack–style stands. Dockside crab and chowder stands are a Juneau tradition for cruise passengers — expect a line at peak times.
  • Downtown cafés. Coffee shops and bakeries are an easy warm-up stop if the weather turns cool or wet.
  • Kids. Simple grill or chowder options beat a fancier seafood restaurant if everyone’s cold and ready to head back.
— Shopping

What’s worth bringing home

  • Alaska Native art. Look for authenticity markers (like the Silver Hand program) if you want genuine Tlingit, Haida, or other Alaska Native work rather than mass-produced souvenirs.
  • Local salmon and smoked seafood. Vacuum-sealed options travel well and make a distinctive souvenir.
  • Cruise-line jewelry stores. The same diamond/tanzanite shops found in other cruise ports line the main strip — fine to browse, not required.
— Safety

With kids in daylight

  • Downtown Juneau is compact, walkable, and generally low-crime in daylight.
  • Weather changes fast — layers matter more than any other single packing decision for this port.
  • Glacier viewpoints and trails can be slick; stick to marked paths and keep an eye on kids near the moraine and water.
  • Whale-watching boats can be choppy — motion-sensitive kids may want a seasickness plan before boarding.

— Don’t miss this

Juneau is one of the easier Alaska ports to DIY because downtown is dockside. Pick one big activity (glacier or whales), not both, unless your call is long — then enjoy the walkable downtown with whatever time is left.

— Don’t miss

The one thing repeats push

See the glacier up close, even briefly. Mendenhall is one of the few glaciers you can reach without a serious hike, and its retreat is visible year to year — it’s the kind of sight that’s genuinely different from anything on a Caribbean itinerary. Pair it with a short downtown walk rather than rushing both a glacier tour and a full whale-watching trip.
