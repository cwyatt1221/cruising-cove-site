Port of call · Alaska

Ketchikan.

Alaska’s rainiest big port — and one of its most rewarding, with Creek Street’s boardwalks, the world’s largest collection of totem poles, and fishing right off the dock.

— The day

Three clean options

Ketchikan splits into Creek Street and downtown, a totem-pole and culture stop, or a fishing or floatplane excursion out over Misty Fjords. Bring rain gear no matter which you pick — this is Alaska’s rainforest port, and dry days are the exception, not the rule.

Low transfer

Creek Street & downtown

Boardwalk buildings on stilts over the creek, small shops, and salmon-viewing platforms during the run — all a short walk from the pier.

Culture stop

Totem parks

Home to the world’s largest collection of totem poles, split across a few sites (some downtown, some a short ride out) with Tlingit, Haida, and Tsimshian heritage.

Signature excursion

Fishing / Misty Fjords

Salmon fishing charters are a Ketchikan classic; floatplane or boat tours into Misty Fjords National Monument run on clearer days.

  • Rain gear beats an umbrella. Ketchikan gets over 150 inches of rain a year — a real rain jacket and waterproof shoes matter more here than anywhere else on the itinerary.
  • Walkable core. Creek Street and much of downtown are steps from the pier, so a rainy day doesn’t have to mean a wasted call.
  • Disney transfer. Worth it for fishing charters or floatplane tours where weather-driven schedule changes make the ship-wait guarantee valuable. See Port Adventures filters.
— Food

What to eat ashore

  • Fresh salmon and halibut. Ketchikan calls itself the salmon capital of the world for good reason — grilled or fried, it’s the local specialty to look for.
  • Creek Street cafés. Small waterfront spots along the boardwalk make a good rainy-day lunch stop with a view of the creek.
  • Fishing-day catch. Some charters offer to have your catch processed and shipped — ask before you book if that matters to you.
  • Kids. Simple chowder or fish-and-chips spots downtown beat waiting out weather for a fancier reservation.
— Shopping

What’s worth bringing home

  • Alaska Native art and carvings. Look for authenticity markers if you want genuine Tlingit, Haida, or Tsimshian work — totem park gift shops are a good place to start.
  • Rain gear (yes, really). If you underpacked, downtown outfitters sell exactly what you need for the rest of the sailing.
  • Cruise-line jewelry chains. Present here as in most Alaska ports — fine to browse, not the local specialty.
— Safety

With kids in daylight

  • Downtown Ketchikan and Creek Street are compact, well-trafficked, and generally very safe in daylight.
  • Wet boardwalks and wooden stairs on Creek Street get slippery — good grip shoes matter more than style.
  • Floatplane and fishing charters are weather-dependent; operators cancel or reroute for safety, so build flexibility into your day rather than fighting the schedule.
  • Layered, waterproof clothing is a safety issue as much as a comfort one — cold, wet kids tire and complain fast.

— Don’t miss this

Ketchikan’s biggest timing risk is weather-driven cancellations, not distance. Book flexible or Disney-run tours for Misty Fjords or fishing days, and keep a rainy-day downtown plan ready so a washed-out excursion doesn’t sink the whole call.

— Don’t miss

The one thing repeats push

Walk Creek Street and see the totem poles — don’t let rain talk you out of it. The boardwalk over the creek and the concentration of totem art here are unlike anything else on a typical itinerary, rain or shine. If the weather cooperates, a fishing charter or a Misty Fjords flight is the extra a lot of repeat visitors say made the stop.
