Panama Canal.

— What it is

A canal day, not a pier day

On Disney’s Panama Canal itineraries, the ship transits the canal — you don’t dock in Panama City for a beach afternoon. Expect a long scenic day of locks, Gatun Lake, and commentary from the decks while the ship moves between the Pacific and Atlantic sides. Port days elsewhere (Mexico, Colombia, Cayman, and so on) are the “go ashore” stops; canal day is the bucket-list spectacle.

Signature day

Full transit

Watch the ship rise and fall through the locks. Bring binoculars, claim a rail early, and plan shade or sunscreen for hours on deck.

Sea days

~8 days at sea

These sailings include a lot of open water. Use them for pools, kids clubs, Palo, and rest — and pack for motion if your family is sensitive.

One-way trip

Different embark & return

You fly into one coast and home from the other. Book flights, hotels, and transfers for both ends before you lock the cruise.

— Before you book

Age, payment & documents

  • Minimum age: Panama Canal repositioning sailings require guests to be at least 1 year old at embarkation (not the usual 6 months). See the FAQ.
  • Final payment: At 14 nights, expect the longer (~120-day) final-payment window typical of week-plus itineraries — confirm on your reservation. See deposit & final payment.
  • Passports: One-way international itineraries almost always mean passports for everyone — verify current Disney document rules for your sailing early.
  • Flights: Book open-jaw (or separate one-ways) to match embark and debark cities. San Diego and Galveston are not interchangeable last-minute.
— Onboard

How to spend canal day

Viewing

Claim deck space early

Forward and upper open decks fill for the locks. Bring water, sunscreen, a light layer for wind, and patience — transit stretches across much of the day.

Kids

Mix viewing + clubs

Young kids rarely want eight continuous hours at the rail. Alternate deck time with kids clubs, pools, and quiet cabin breaks.

Photo tip

Binoculars help

Locks, tugboats, and shoreline details are far enough that a small pair of binoculars earns its suitcase space — same tip as Alaska wildlife days.

— What to pack

Warm ports + long sea days

Think Caribbean beach kit plus extras for open-ocean days and canal viewing — not Alaska layers. Full destination notes live on the packing list.

  • Reef-safe sunscreen, hats, and lightweight cover-ups for Mexico / Cayman / Colombia heat
  • Motion remedies for multi-day ocean stretches
  • Binoculars for canal and wildlife viewing
  • Comfortable walking shoes for Cartagena / city stops
  • Passport wallet setup for both embark and return airports
— Priority

The one thing this sailing is for

Be on deck for the locks. Everything else — beach clubs, old-city walks, sea-day Palo — is bonus. If you sleep through the first lock sequence, you’ve missed the reason families book a Panama Canal cruise.
