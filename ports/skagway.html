Port of call · Alaska

Skagway.

A Gold Rush-era town small enough to walk end to end, with the White Pass & Yukon Route train as the classic reason to book ahead.

— The day

Three clean options

Skagway is one of the most compact, walkable ports on an Alaska itinerary. Most calls come down to the train, a wider Klondike-area tour, or a slow downtown walk through preserved Gold Rush storefronts. The historic district sits steps from the pier.

Signature excursion

White Pass & Yukon Route

A narrow-gauge railway climbing steep grades toward the Yukon border, with scenery that draws people back to Skagway specifically for the ride. Book ahead — it sells out.

Bigger loop

Klondike Highway / Yukon day

Van or coach tours push farther into the mountains than the train alone, often combining scenery with a stop at a summit or lake.

Low transfer

Downtown Gold Rush walk

Wooden boardwalks, preserved 1898-era buildings, and small museums — all within a few blocks of where the ship docks.

  • Walk-everything port. Unlike most cruise stops, you may not need a taxi at all if downtown and the train depot are your day.
  • Book the train early. The White Pass & Yukon Route is Skagway’s single most in-demand activity — popular departure times fill up well before the sailing.
  • Disney transfer. Worth considering for Klondike Highway or Yukon-area tours that go farther from town. See Port Adventures filters.
— Food

What to eat ashore

  • Downtown cafés and bakeries. Small, walkable options serve chowder, sandwiches, and reindeer sausage — easy for a short window.
  • Historic saloons. A few of the preserved Gold Rush-era buildings house restaurants and bars leaning into the theme — fun for a photo stop and a meal.
  • Train day. Some White Pass departures are timed tightly with the ship — grab food before or after, not mid-tour.
  • Kids. Simple sandwich and soup spots downtown beat trying to squeeze in a sit-down meal on a train-day schedule.
— Shopping

What’s worth bringing home

  • Gold Rush and local history items. Skagway leans hard into its 1898 heritage — books, prints, and small museum-shop items make a more distinctive souvenir than generic cruise trinkets.
  • Alaska-made goods. Local art, jewelry, and food items are sold alongside the same cruise-line jewelry chains found in other ports.
  • Small footprint. Downtown is compact enough that browsing the whole main street takes well under an hour — easy to combine with the train or a walk.
— Safety

With kids in daylight

  • Downtown Skagway is small, walkable, and generally very low-crime in daylight.
  • Wooden boardwalks and uneven historic-district surfaces can be slippery when wet — good shoes matter more than fashion here.
  • Train routes run along steep grades and cliffside views — keep kids seated and away from open vestibule areas per crew instructions.
  • Weather shifts quickly in the mountains; dress in layers even if the morning looks mild at the pier.

— Don’t miss this

Skagway is forgiving on timing compared to most Alaska stops — the harder problem is booking the train early enough, not fitting it into your day. Reserve your seat as soon as your sailing’s excursions open.

— Don’t miss

The one thing repeats push

Ride the White Pass & Yukon Route at least once. The climb out of Skagway past waterfalls, trestles, and the summit views is a genuinely different kind of scenery than anything else on a typical Disney itinerary. If the train is sold out, a slow walk through the preserved downtown is still worth the stop.
