Door décor trend

Disney cruise door banners.

Small magnets still work — but full-door personalized banners are the new hallway flex. Here’s what they are, what DCL still allows, and which doors won’t hold them.

Published September 18, 2026 · Door décor · Disney Cruise Line

For years, Disney cruise door décor meant a scatter of character magnets, a countdown sign, maybe a family name strip. That tradition isn’t going away — but a newer look is taking over: a single full-door banner that covers most of the cabin face with one personalized design.

Think Hercules columns, Haunted Mansion gothic frames, Tangled braids, Star Wars Endor scenes, or a simple “Oh Ship — it’s our first cruise” print with your family name. One piece. Instant landmark in a hallway of identical doors.

Quick facts

🧲 Still magnets-only — no tape, Command strips, gel adhesives, or over-the-door hangers.

🚪 Décor stays on the door only (not hallway walls or ceilings) under the June 2026 rule.

💵 Damage from prohibited attachments can mean a $100 fee per incident.

📏 Stateroom doors are roughly 28″ × 77″; many printable templates run about 24″ × 72″.

What a door banner actually is

Sellers and DIYers use a few formats under the same “banner” label:

  • Full-height fabric or vinyl prints with magnets sewn in or magnetic sheet on the back — the look that covers nearly the whole door.
  • Smaller magnetic fabric flags (often around 12″ × 18″) with family names and sayings — easier to pack, still more cohesive than a pile of tiny magnets.
  • Canva / print-shop templates you customize, print, laminate or mount, then back with magnets yourself.

The shared idea: one design that carries your theme and name, instead of arranging twenty separate magnets every embarkation afternoon.

DCL rules still apply

Disney welcomes tasteful magnetic door personalization — and updated the policy so decorations stay on the stateroom door only. Corridor walls and ceilings are off-limits. Sound or video on the door is a no. Valuables shouldn’t hang in the hall.

The attachment rule that matters for banners: magnets, not adhesives. If your banner needs tape or Command strips to stay up, it’s the wrong setup for DCL. Use strong magnets or a magnetic backing, and leave the official room-number plate visible so crew can still find you.

Full policy context lives on our door decorations guide.

Wooden doors: Dream & Fantasy Concierge

Most Disney stateroom doors are metal and hold magnets well — including Magic and Wonder (Concierge included).

The exception: Concierge staterooms on the Disney Dream and Disney Fantasy have a wood veneer finish. Standard fridge magnets often won’t stick; some guests use strong neodymium magnets through the veneer, but many Concierge hallways stay mostly undecorated.

If you’re booked Concierge on Dream or Fantasy, check recent sailing reports — or plan a Fish Extender and skip relying on a full magnetic banner.

Why banners are catching on

  • Less fuss at embarkation — hang one piece instead of arranging a collage.
  • Easier to find your room after a long port day.
  • Photo-ready — family name and theme read clearly in hallway pics.
  • Fits the door-only rule — creativity stays on the cabin face, not spilled onto walls.
  • Packs flat — fabric banners often roll or fold into a suitcase.

Where to get one

Etsy is packed with personalized Disney cruise door banners and magnetic fabric signs. Custom print shops and Canva templates (often sized near 24″ × 72″) work if you want to DIY and add your own magnet backing. Confirm magnet strength before you sail — hallway doors shut hard.

Pack the banner in your carry-on so you can decorate as soon as you get the room, before checked bags arrive.
