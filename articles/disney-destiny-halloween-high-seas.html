Halloween · Disney Destiny

Halloween on the High Seas comes to the Disney Destiny.

What not to miss on Destiny’s first Halloween season — and what’s worth building your days around.

Published August 3, 2026 · Disney Destiny · Halloween

For the first time since setting sail, the Disney Destiny is joining Halloween on the High Seas for the 2026 season — one of seven ships across the fleet hosting the celebration this year. If you’re booking a fall sailing on Disney’s newest ship, here’s what to expect and what’s worth building your days around.

What Halloween on the High Seas actually is

It’s not a separate ticketed event — it’s a themed overlay built into select September and October sailings, similar in format to Star Wars Day at Sea. Book one of the eligible cruises and the Halloween programming comes with it: decor, character costumes, themed food, and a schedule of seasonal entertainment layered on top of the ship’s usual offerings. The Destiny’s fall lineup includes 7-night sailings out of Fort Lauderdale, making it one of the longer Halloween-season options in the fleet this year — and a good fit if you’d rather have a full week of it instead of a quick 3- or 4-night dose.

Don’t miss: the Pumpkin Tree lighting on night one

Every ship’s atrium gets its own signature Pumpkin Tree for the season, and each one comes with its own name and backstory revealed by the tree’s “caretaker” during a storytelling ceremony on the first night. Once the jack-o-lanterns are lit, the tree stays glowing for the rest of the cruise as the go-to photo backdrop. The Destiny’s Pumpkin Tree hadn’t been officially named as of this writing, but it’s a safe bet Disney will lean into the ship’s hero-and-villain theming to give it its own personality — worth checking the Navigator app on embarkation day for the reveal.

Don’t miss: Mickey’s Mouse-querade Party

The signature event of the whole celebration is a deck party featuring Mickey and friends in their Halloween costumes, with dancing, music, and games. It’s typically scheduled for a sea day so you’re not torn between the party and a port excursion, and it doubles as an easy way to see your favorite characters in their seasonal looks without standing in a meet-and-greet line.

Don’t miss: onboard trick-or-treating

Kids can trick-or-treat right on the ship, following a designated trail through public spaces to collect treats — no need to bring your own bag, since treat bags are typically left in staterooms ahead of time. It’s one of the most talked-about parts of the whole event for families, and something to check the daily Navigator schedule for so you don’t miss the window.

Don’t miss: Halloween character costumes you’ll only see this time of year

Mickey, Minnie, and friends turn up in costumes that are exclusive to the Halloween season — often witch- or wizard-themed looks, and on some ships, Minnie, Daisy, and Clarabelle Cow appear dressed as the Sanderson Sisters from Hocus Pocus. Whether that particular experience makes it to the Destiny hasn’t been confirmed yet, but seasonal character appearances are a fleet-wide staple, and exact times and locations show up in the Navigator app once you’re onboard.

Don’t miss: the seasonal menu

Halloween flavors show up across the ship for the whole sailing — think pumpkin spice treats, spooky-themed desserts, and specialty items at the buffet, sweet shop, and select lounges. On the night of Mickey’s Mouse-querade, main dining rooms typically serve a festive dessert as part of the meal. If you have a sweet tooth, this is worth pacing yourself for rather than filling up early in the cruise.

Don’t miss: Pirate Night, if it’s already on your itinerary

Halloween on the High Seas sailings that would normally include Pirate Night keep it on the schedule, so you may get both costume-forward nights on the same cruise. Good news for anyone packing for the trip — a pirate costume and a Halloween costume can often be the same effort with a small wardrobe swap.

Don’t miss: Halloween movies in the ship’s theater

Expect a rotating lineup of seasonal favorites in the onboard theater throughout the sailing — titles across the fleet’s Halloween programming have included Hocus Pocus, The Nightmare Before Christmas, Coco, The Haunted Mansion, Frankenweenie, and family-friendly picks like Toy Story of Terror. A good option for a lower-key evening, especially with younger kids who’ve had a full day of parties and trick-or-treating.

What to pack

Costumes are optional but common — plenty of guests stay in character for the whole cruise, not just the party. Disney’s own guidance leans toward playful and family-friendly: something comfortable enough to dance, walk around the ship, and trick-or-treat in, rather than anything elaborate or restrictive. If you’re bringing a costume for your child, check Disney Cruise Line’s official costume guidelines before you sail, since there are some restrictions (no full face masks for guests old enough to walk the trick-or-treat trail unassisted, for example, on past sailings).

One thing worth knowing before you book

Because this is the Destiny’s first year hosting Halloween on the High Seas, some specifics that are well-documented on ships like the Wish, Fantasy, or Dream — like whether the Destiny gets its own version of interactive extras such as the Wish’s “Who’s Behind the Curtain?” experience, or exactly how the Pumpkin Tree ties into the ship’s heroes-and-villains theme — aren’t confirmed yet. The core lineup (Pumpkin Tree lighting, Mouse-querade, trick-or-treating, seasonal characters and menus) is essentially guaranteed since it’s fleet-wide, but the Destiny-specific flourishes are still an open question. Worth checking the Navigator app close to your sail date, since Disney tends to firm up ship-specific details as the season approaches.

Bottom line: book an eligible fall Destiny sailing and the Halloween overlay is included — plan night one around the Pumpkin Tree, keep a sea-day window open for Mouse-querade, and use Navigator for trick-or-treat and character times.
