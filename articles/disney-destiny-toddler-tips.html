Sailing the Disney Destiny with a toddler.

What parents actually need to know — beyond the usual nursery and kids’ club rundown — once you’re onboard with a two-year-old in tow.

Published August 3, 2026 · Disney Destiny · Families

The Disney Destiny is the newest ship in the fleet, and if you’ve searched for toddler-specific tips, you’ve probably noticed most of what’s out there is really just a rundown of the nursery and kids’ club — the same info repeated across a dozen sites. That’s useful as a starting point, but it doesn’t answer the questions that actually matter once you’re on the ship with a two-year-old in tow. Here’s what we know so far, pulled together in one place.

1. Book the nursery the second your window opens

“It’s a Small World” Nursery covers ages 6 months to 3 years and isn’t included in your cruise fare — expect to pay around $10 an hour with a one-hour minimum. On the Destiny it’s located on Deck 2, near the Oceaneer Club. Reservations open based on your Castaway Club status, and spots are limited enough that they fill fast, especially for peak dinner hours and sea days. If you have your heart set on an adults-only dinner or a spa afternoon, this is the first thing to lock in.

Use the booking windows tool to see when nursery and other reservations typically open for your sailing and tier.

2. There’s no in-room babysitting — the nursery is it

If your toddler isn’t old enough for the Oceaneer Club (ages 3 and up), the nursery is your only childcare option on the ship. There’s no service that sends a sitter to your stateroom, so any adult time has to be built around nursery hours and availability.

3. Toddlers get their own playroom, separate from the nursery

Mickey & Minnie Captain’s Deck is the Destiny’s toddler play space, themed around a ship’s bridge with a slide, tunnels, and low-to-the-ground interactive elements. Nursery-enrolled toddlers get dedicated access at select times, and the space also hosts “Minnie’s Captain Academy,” a high-energy session built around games, dancing, and playful maritime drills. It’s worth noting this description comes from press previews rather than parent trip reports, so how well it holds up with a full room of toddlers — or how a shyer 18-month-old does with the energy level — is still an open question.

4. Swim diapers get you into the splash zone, not the pools

Toddlers in swim diapers can use the designated splash zone, but the main pools are potty-trained only — that’s a U.S. Public Health rule, not a Disney one, so there’s no way around it. Pack swim diapers regardless of how confident your child is in the regular ones.

5. Leave the full-size stroller at home

Staterooms and hallways are tight, and a compact, foldable umbrella stroller that tucks under the bed or into a closet will serve you far better than a full-size jogger. Guest Services also has a limited number of complimentary strollers if you’d rather not bring your own. Be ready to fold it up on request during meals or in tighter spaces.

6. Request a pack ’n play and Diaper Genie before you board

Both are available through your stateroom host on request, the same as at Disney’s resorts — just ask ahead of time or as soon as you check in.

7. Book the earliest dinner seating if bedtime is a priority

The first rotational dining seating lets a toddler eat and get to bed at a reasonable hour instead of a 8:15 pm seating. And if a meltdown happens at the table anyway, cast members will pack your meal to go so you can finish it back in the stateroom.

8. Naptime is genuinely protected

Ship-wide announcements only play into staterooms on embarkation afternoon. After that, nothing gets piped in unless it’s an emergency — so a nap won’t get interrupted by a PA announcement. Check the Navigator app instead of the room speaker for daily updates.

9. Walk the kids’ areas on embarkation day, before they’re kids-only

On embarkation day, families can tour all the children’s spaces before they become off-limits to adults for the rest of the cruise. It’s a good window to let a toddler get familiar with the room and meet staff before a first nursery drop-off, when the space is calmer than during a busy open house.

10. The onboard shop has backup supplies, but bring the comfort items

Shops carry a small selection of diapers, wipes, ready-made formula, pacifiers, and sunscreen, so a forgotten item isn’t a full crisis. What the shop can’t replace is a familiar blanket or stuffed animal — worth prioritizing in your carry-on for an overtired toddler in an unfamiliar space.

11. The walk to Lookout Cay is longer than it looks — and there’s no shade

If your itinerary stops at Disney’s Lookout Cay at Lighthouse Point, know this before you disembark: there’s currently no shuttle for most guests between the ship and the start of the island (only those needing accessibility assistance can request a golf cart from Guest Services). Everyone else walks, and there’s no shade along that stretch of pier. One parent put it plainly: there was no way to get a toddler to make that walk on their own two feet, there and back.

Once you reach the Mabrika Cove tram stop, a free shuttle takes you the rest of the way to the Goombay area, where the beaches, restaurants, and activities are — that ride runs about 15 minutes each way, with waits possible at busy times, and both tram stops have shaded pavilions. Complimentary wagons and strollers are available at the Goombay stop on a first-come, first-served basis, but they only help once you’re already there. For the walk from the ship, you’ll want your own compact stroller — and you’ll need to be able to fold it to get it onto the tram.

Bottom line: if you’re bringing a toddler to Lookout Cay, plan on using your own foldable stroller for the walk from the ship, not the loaners. Comparing islands? See Castaway Cay vs. Lookout Cay.

12. Nursery communication works differently at Lookout Cay

Because the ship anchors farther from shore at Lookout Cay than it does at Castaway Cay, the in-room phone system doesn’t reliably reach the nursery while you’re on the island. If you need to check in or the nursery needs to reach you, don’t count on a normal phone call going through. One workaround that’s worked for other families: head to Sebastian’s Cove, the kids’ club on the island itself, and ask a crew member there to relay a message to the nursery by walkie-talkie. It’s not instant, but it works.
