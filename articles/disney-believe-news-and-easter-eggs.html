Disney Believe: every reveal so far — and the easter eggs.

D23 Horizons finally put a face on the fifth Wish-class ship — Moana in the Grand Hall, Woody and Buzz on the stern, and a 1953 Tinker Bell on the bow.

August 16, 2026 · Ships

Disney’s fifth Wish-class ship finally has a face. After months of near-silence, Disney Cruise Line used the D23 Horizons showcase on August 15, 2026 to unveil the first real artwork for the Disney Believe — and tucked inside that reveal are exactly the kind of nostalgic details DCL fans live to spot.

Here’s everything confirmed so far, and the easter eggs worth knowing about before the ship even touches water. For the ongoing ship guide (capacity assumptions, Wish-class planning notes), start with Disney Believe.

The basics

  • Name revealed: March 18, 2026, by Josh D’Amaro at Disney’s Annual Shareholders Meeting
  • Sail date: Late 2027
  • Class: Fourth ship in the Wish class, joining Disney Wish, Disney Treasure, and Disney Destiny
  • Builder: Meyer Werft, Papenburg, Germany (hull number 723 — Meyer’s 57th cruise ship overall, its 7th for Disney)
  • Theme: “Promise and possibility” — stories about characters who believe in themselves

The stories confirmed to shape the ship’s design and entertainment: Encanto, Frozen, Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs, Moana, and The Little Mermaid.

What D23 actually showed

The August 15 panel, led by Portfolio Executive Creative Director Claire Weiss, gave the first real look at three signature art pieces — bow, stern, and Grand Hall — following the tradition every DCL ship observes.

Grand Hall: Moana. The ship’s central gathering space takes its cue from Disney Animation’s Moana films. A bronze statue of Moana, flanked by Pua and Heihei, anchors the room, and Imagineers layered in artisanal Polynesian finishes referencing the film’s South Pacific setting.

Stern: Woody and Buzz Lightyear. Instead of the looping calligraphy DCL normally stamps across a ship’s stern, the Disney Believe swaps it out for a Toy Story gag — larger-than-life sculptures of Woody and Buzz appear to be etching the ship’s name themselves, using oversized yellow crayons, in a blocky font styled after the Toy Story logo.

Bow: Tinker Bell. A hand-painted filigree of Tinker Bell exactly as she appeared in 1953’s original Peter Pan, surrounded by silhouettes of Peter, Wendy, Captain Hook, and other characters from the film swirling around her.

The easter eggs

A few details are worth calling out specifically for anyone tracking DCL’s usual visual language:

  • The broken calligraphy tradition. Every other Wish-class ship uses the same looped script for its stern lettering. The Believe is the first to break that pattern, in favor of Woody and Buzz’s crayon scrawl — a nod that the artwork is the easter egg here, not just decoration around it.
  • A 1953-accurate Tink. Disney specifically used Tinker Bell’s original 1953 animated design for the bow art rather than a modern redesign, which fan sites have flagged as a deliberate throwback for classic-animation purists.
  • Companions get top billing. Both Pua and Heihei appear alongside Moana in the bronze Grand Hall statue — consistent with DCL’s pattern of including sidekick characters in atrium statuary (Olaf with Anna and Elsa, etc.), rather than a solo hero pose.

What’s coming next

Disney has set October 7, 2026 for a dedicated livestream reveal with more Disney Believe details. Guests already booked on the Disney Wish’s October 5–9, 2026 sailing get an early piece of it: a one-night-only stage show previewing new Believe experiences, plus a commemorative gift in every stateroom on that sailing.

Itineraries, specific venues beyond the Grand Hall, and full dining and entertainment lineups haven’t been announced yet — worth checking back after the October livestream, and keeping the Believe ship guide bookmarked for updates. The same D23 weekend also confirmed a new ship class arriving from 2029.
