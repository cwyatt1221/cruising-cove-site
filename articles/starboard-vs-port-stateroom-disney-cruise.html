Starboard vs port: which side of the ship should you book?

Castaway Cay views, Pirate Night fireworks, and the dock-side wave goodbye — what’s true, what’s a maybe, and what doesn’t depend on side at all.

Published August 30, 2026 · Cabin intel · Castaway Cay

Once you’ve picked a ship and a stateroom category, Disney Cruise Line asks for one more detail: port or starboard? On a map, starboard is the right side when you’re facing the bow (front). Port is the left.

For most Bahamian and Caribbean sailings, the debate boils down to three moments: waking up at Castaway Cay, watching Pirate Night fireworks, and the emotional dock-side goodbye when the ship pulls away from the island. You’ve probably heard starboard wins on all three. The real answer is more nuanced — and worth knowing before you pay a premium for one side.

Quick verdict

  • Book starboard if your priority is a Castaway Cay beach view from your verandah or porthole — it’s the usual winner when Disney backs into the pier.
  • Book port if you love pier activity — gangways, tenders, and Cast Members waving from the dock when the ship arrives and departs.
  • Don’t pick a side for fireworks alone — launch side can change for wind, and many verandahs get safety notices anyway. Deck 11–12 is still the reliable plan.
  • On Lookout Cay, Nassau, and most home ports, side is much less predictable. Castaway Cay is the one port where Disney’s pattern is well established.

Fact-check: your starboard claims

“The ships back into Castaway Cay, so starboard gets the better view”

Mostly true — with an asterisk. Disney Cruise Line ships typically back into the Castaway Cay pier, which puts the developed beach side of the island along the starboard hull. planDisney and long-running cruise guides describe this as the standard Castaway Cay setup.

That means a starboard verandah or oceanview is your best shot at waking up to turquoise water, umbrellas, and the main beach — instead of the pier or the less-developed side of the island.

Caveat: Disney does not guarantee docking orientation. Weather, pier logistics, or operational needs can force a different approach. Treat starboard as the best odds, not a contract.

“Fireworks are on the starboard side”

True for where they’re launched — not always true for where you should watch. On Pirate Night, Disney’s at-sea fireworks (Buccaneer Blast on most ships; a similar show on Wish-class) are typically launched from the starboard/aft area of the ship.

Some forward starboard verandahs can catch a private-feeling view. But midship and aft starboard balconies are often the ones that receive safety notices asking guests to stay inside during the show, because pyrotechnics are fired close to the hull.

Disney may also switch launch side for wind. Veteran cruisers report occasional port-side launches on windy nights. And here’s the twist: from the pool deck, many families prefer the port side because you can see both the stage show and the fireworks in one sightline — starboard gets the bursts but misses a lot of the party.

Bottom line: starboard is where the fireworks usually come from; it is not automatically the best stateroom for watching them.

Fact-check: your port-side claim

“Port side is where Disney workers wave goodbye”

Yes — this is the port-side sweet spot. When the ship backs into Castaway Cay, the pier and gangway are on the port side. That’s the side you disembark from, and it’s the side that faces the dock all day.

From a port verandah you can watch the arrival choreography: lines handled, gangways set, guests streaming ashore. When the ship leaves in the late afternoon, Cast Members on the pier often line up in Mickey gloves to wave guests off — a small moment kids remember, and port-side rooms overlook it directly.

Starboard guests get the island postcard view instead. Port guests get the show on the pier. Neither is wrong; they’re different cameras on the same day.

Other starboard benefits worth knowing

  • Castaway Cay morning coffee. The classic “palm trees from my verandah” photo is overwhelmingly a starboard shot on standard docking days.
  • Less pier infrastructure in frame. Port side at Castaway Cay often looks at the dock, service areas, or the undeveloped shoreline — still pretty, but more industrial than beachy.
  • Potential in-room fireworks — if you’re picky about room number. Forward starboard verandahs sometimes work; aft starboard may be restricted. Read deck plans and forum reports for your exact category.
  • Same logic can apply at Lookout Cay — sometimes. Disney’s newer private island doesn’t follow Castaway’s pattern as reliably. Some sailings dock with the island on port instead. If your itinerary includes both islands, don’t assume starboard wins twice.

Other port benefits worth knowing

  • Dock-side people-watching. Arrival, departure, and the wave line are all port-side theater when Disney backs in at Castaway Cay.
  • The Mickey-glove goodbye. When the ship pulls away in the afternoon, Cast Members often line the pier in white gloves — port verandahs get the full send-off (your kids will remember it).
  • Closest to the gangway at Castaway Cay. You disembark on the port side. Midship port rooms are a short walk to the pier — handy if you want to be in the first wave ashore or duck back to the ship for sunscreen, snacks, or a nap without a long hike through the ship.
  • Watch the ship back in. If you’re an early riser on island day, port-side rooms overlook the docking maneuver itself — tugs, lines, and the slow reverse into the pier before the beaches open.
  • Time your return from the island. Port-side guests can literally watch the gangway line from the balcony. When the queue shrinks before the all-aboard call, you’ll know it without guessing from the beach.
  • Pirate Night from the pool deck. Fireworks usually launch starboard/aft, but the deck party happens center-pool. Many families prefer the port side of Deck 11–12 so one spot frames both the show and the sky bursts.
  • Lookout Cay wild card. When Disney docks with the island on the port side at Lookout Cay, port rooms get the beach view that starboard guests wanted at Castaway Cay. Side picks don’t transfer between islands.
  • Port Canaveral sail-away — NASA on the horizon. Leaving from Disney’s Terminal 8, many cruisers spot the Kennedy Space Center Vehicle Assembly Building (the giant NASA hangar) from the port side once the ship clears the channel and turns toward open water. It’s ~15 miles away — a distant rectangle on the Cape, not a close-up — but kids who love space will lose their minds. Grab binoculars and head to your verandah right after sail-away begins.
  • Embarkation-day curiosity. At some home ports, one side faces the terminal or parking structure while the other faces open water — useful if you care about the view while waiting to sail. Port Canaveral assignments vary by pier and ship; there’s no universal port/starboard rule here.

    • When side barely matters

    • Inside staterooms — no window, no debate.
    • Alaska, Europe, and most long-haul itineraries — no Pirate Night fireworks at sea; port calls change daily.
    • Nassau and many third-party ports — ships can dock bow-in, stern-in, or tender depending on pier assignment. Either side can face the harbor — or a warehouse wall.
    • Motion and noise — midship low beats port vs starboard for seasickness and elevator rumble. See cabin intel for ship-specific noise patterns.
    • Sunrise and sunset — depend on sailing direction and time of year more than port/starboard. A eastbound Bahamas loop won’t follow the same light as a one-way Panama Canal crossing.

    Lookout Cay: don’t copy-paste your Castaway Cay pick

    Lookout Cay at Lighthouse Point is still newer in the rotation, and Disney has docked ships facing both directions depending on schedule, weather, and logistics. planDisney has described it as less predictable than Castaway Cay — with educated guesses based on the prior port, not guarantees.

    If your sailing hits both private islands, you may get starboard-facing beach views one day and port-facing the next. That’s a strong argument for not overpaying for side unless Castaway Cay is the only island day you care about. Compare both in our Castaway Cay vs Lookout Cay guide.

    How to actually choose

    1. List your “must see from the room” moments. Beach sunrise at Castaway Cay → lean starboard. Pier goodbye line → lean port.
    2. Prioritize deck and room number over side. A forward starboard verandah with an obstructed view loses to a midship port with a clear sightline. Use stateroom guides and deck plans before you assign a side.
    3. Plan fireworks on Deck 12. Even starboard guests with perfect balconies may be asked to stay inside. The show is short — watch it where Disney intends.
    4. Let your agent know preferences, not demands. Side is a request category, not a specific cabin. A good travel agent can hunt starboard verandahs in the right deck range; Disney assigns exact numbers later.
    5. Keep expectations flexible. The ship will still get you to the island. The beach is the same no matter which hull faces it.
