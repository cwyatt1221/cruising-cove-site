What is Disney Uncharted Adventure?

The free, phone-based scavenger hunt on Wish-class ships — the Midship Detective Agency’s spiritual successor, played through the Disney Cruise Line Navigator app.

Published August 20, 2026 · Onboard activities · Wish · Treasure · Destiny

Disney Uncharted Adventure is a free interactive experience on the Disney Wish, Disney Treasure, and Disney Destiny. Your phone becomes a “magical spyglass” that triggers artwork, props, and story moments as you roam the ship on quests to restore the Wishing Star.

If you’ve sailed the Dream or Fantasy and loved the Midship Detective Agency, this is the Wish-class version of that idea — same “explore the ship and make the walls come alive” energy, different technology.

How it differs from Midship Detective Agency

  • Dream / Fantasy: physical detective badge and clue cards at an atrium kiosk.
  • Wish / Treasure / Destiny: everything runs inside the Disney Cruise Line Navigator app — no paper cards.
  • Both are free, self-paced, and excellent for learning a confusing deck layout on a sea day.

How to play

  1. Download the Disney Cruise Line Navigator app before you sail and sign in with your Disney account / cruise reservation when available.
  2. Once onboard (and connected to ship Wi‑Fi or cellular), open Uncharted Adventure in the app.
  3. Create or use your DisneyMe profile if prompted, then pick a quest.
  4. Follow on-screen directions to locations around the ship.
  5. Hold your phone up like a spyglass toward the marked artwork, signs, or props so the experience can trigger AR / interactive effects.
  6. Complete mini-challenges, earn achievements, and move to the next stop until the quest wraps.

You can pause for dinner, a show, or nap time and pick up later — there’s no single forced session.

What kinds of quests you’ll see

Quests pull from Disney and Pixar worlds. Common themes guests report include places like Never Land, Motunui (Moana), and the Great Barrier Reef with Nemo and friends. Exact quest lists can rotate, and Destiny also layers in Avengers-flavored adventures alongside the shared Wish-class set.

Many sailings also build toward a group finale event (often billed around battling for / restoring the Wishing Star) in a theater or entertainment venue — check Navigator for the night and time on your cruise.

Why families like it

It’s included

No reservation fee and no ticket — just your phone and the app.

It teaches the ship

Wish-class decks confuse a lot of first-timers. Quests deliberately send you bow to stern, which is one of the fastest ways to stop getting lost between the Grand Hall, Oceaneer, and the pool deck.

It’s flexible

Play one quest, play several, or skip the finale. Kids who burn out on AR can hand the phone to a parent and still walk the route together.

Insider tips

Download Navigator before embarkation day. Hotel Wi‑Fi and cellular are easier than fighting the ship network while you’re trying to board.

Bring a charged phone (or a battery pack). Spyglass mode plus photos and Navigator notifications drains battery fast on a sea day.

Play on a sea day or embarkation afternoon. Port mornings are rushed; evenings compete with shows and Worlds of Marvel.

Keep your head up between triggers. Disney designs this as a “heads-up” hunt — the phone is the tool, not the whole vacation. Watch for other families clustered around a mural; that’s usually an active station.

Check the finale night early. If your sailing has a Battle for the Wishing Star–style show, put it on the family calendar so you’re not still finishing a quest when the doors open.

Which ships have which game?

  • Wish, Treasure, Destiny → Disney Uncharted Adventure
  • Dream, Fantasy → Midship Detective Agency
  • Magic / Wonder → neither of these full interactive mystery games (still great for Hidden Mickey hunts and easter eggs)
