Disney cruise booking & cost: your questions answered.

Planning a Disney cruise starts with one big question: what’s this actually going to cost, and how do you book it the smart way? Here’s what you need to know.

Updated August 4, 2026 · Booking & cost

How much does a Disney cruise cost, and what’s included?

Your fare covers your stateroom, all main dining room meals, buffet and quick-service food, snacks throughout the day, room service, entertainment (including Broadway-style stage shows), pools, and access to kids’ programming for ages 3–17. Pricing varies widely based on ship, itinerary length, season, and stateroom category — a short 3-night Bahamian cruise in an inside cabin will cost far less than a 7-night Caribbean sailing in a concierge suite.

What’s not included: alcohol, specialty dining (like Palo or Remy), spa treatments, the nursery for babies under 3, excursions at ports of call, and onboard photos.

For real dollar ranges, see our cost breakdown and gratuities guide.

Should I book directly with Disney or through a travel agent?

Booking through a travel agent who specializes in Disney costs you nothing extra — Disney pays the agent’s commission, not you. In exchange, you typically get a knowledgeable planner who can help you navigate stateroom categories, dining reservations, and add-ons, plus many agencies offer onboard credit as a booking incentive. If you’d rather have full control and don’t mind researching everything yourself, booking directly with Disney works fine too.

Read when an agent actually helps, or browse specialists.

Is travel insurance worth it?

For most families, yes. Cruises involve a lot of moving pieces — flights, hotel stays, and a fixed departure time you can’t miss. Travel insurance protects you against trip cancellation, medical emergencies at sea, and missed connections that could otherwise cost you the entire cruise fare.

What discounts or onboard credit can I get?

Discounts vary by season and are announced periodically by Disney directly. Separately, many travel agencies offer their own onboard credit as an incentive for booking through them — this credit can be used for excursions, specialty dining, spa treatments, or drinks once you’re onboard. It’s worth comparing a few agencies before booking to see who offers the best incentive.

Do kids sail free or at a reduced rate?

Disney doesn’t typically offer a blanket “kids sail free” deal, but children (especially very young ones) are usually priced significantly lower than adults sharing the same stateroom, and infants under 6 months generally aren’t permitted to sail at all on most itineraries. Pricing structures change by promotion, so it’s worth checking current fares for your specific sailing.
