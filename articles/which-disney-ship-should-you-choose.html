Which Disney ship should you choose?

One of the first decisions in planning a Disney cruise is picking the right ship. Here’s how they compare.

Updated August 4, 2026 · Ships & itineraries

How many ships does Disney Cruise Line have, and what’s different about each?

Disney’s fleet has grown over the years and now includes both classic and newer ships, each with its own personality:

  • Disney Magic and Disney Wonder — the original ships, smaller and more intimate, both refreshed while keeping a classic feel.
  • Disney Dream and Disney Fantasy — mid-sized ships known for the AquaDuck water coaster and a great balance of family and adult spaces.
  • Disney Wish, Disney Treasure, and Disney Destiny — the newest generation (Wish class), larger with more elaborate theming, expanded dining, and more onboard activities. Disney Believe joins that class in late 2027.
  • Disney Adventure — Disney’s first Asia-based ship (Singapore), largest in the fleet, with a land-based dining and entertainment map that differs from Wish-class rotational dining.

Generally, newer and larger ships offer more variety in entertainment and dining, while the classic ships tend to feel cozier and easier to navigate, especially with younger kids. Browse the full fleet: ship guides.

Which Disney ship is best for my family?

It depends on what you’re prioritizing:

  • First-time cruisers or families with very young kids: The Dream or Fantasy hit a sweet spot — big enough for variety, not overwhelming to navigate.
  • Families wanting the newest, most elaborate ship: Wish, Treasure, or Destiny offer the latest theming and features.
  • Couples or smaller families wanting something more intimate: Magic or Wonder tend to feel less crowded.

That said, most planners recommend picking your itinerary and travel dates first, then choosing whichever ship happens to sail that route — the differences between ships matter less than getting the right destinations and timing for your trip. Compare Wish vs Treasure in depth: Wish vs Treasure.

What’s the difference between stateroom categories?

Disney staterooms generally fall into four tiers:

  • Inside staterooms — no window, most budget-friendly.
  • Ocean view — a window or porthole, no balcony.
  • Verandah — a private balcony, most popular for families who want fresh air access.
  • Concierge — the top tier, with extra amenities, a dedicated concierge team, and access to exclusive lounges and sun deck areas.

Price jumps between categories vary by ship — on the older Magic and Wonder, the price difference between tiers tends to be more pronounced, while newer ships often have a smaller gap between categories. See stateroom guides and cabin intel before you lock a number.
