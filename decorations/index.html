Onboard traditions

Decorating your stateroom door.

A beloved DCL tradition, with a real rule change as of June 2026. Here's exactly what's allowed now.

— What changed

The June 2026 rule update

Disney Cruise Line updated its door decoration policy on May 28, 2026, effective for sailings starting June 3, 2026. The core change: decorations now have to stay on your stateroom door only — no more spilling onto the hallway walls or ceiling around your door, which some guests had been doing more and more in recent years.

Everything else about the tradition is unchanged. Magnetic decorations are still fully allowed, fish extenders are unaffected, and this remains one of the most fun, low-cost ways to personalize your stateroom before you even step inside.

— The rules, plainly

What's allowed and what's not

  • Magnets: yes. DCL stateroom doors are magnetic surfaces (with one exception below), and character magnets, signs, and displays are the standard way to decorate.
  • Tape, adhesives, hooks, suction cups: no. Anything that isn't magnetic risks damaging the door.
  • Door only: yes. Hallway walls and ceiling: no. This is the new rule as of June 2026.
  • Sound or video elements: no, as a courtesy to other guests in the corridor.
  • Over-the-door organizers: no — these are explicitly prohibited.
  • Damage a door decorating it improperly, and you're looking at a $100 fee per incident.
— Worth knowing

Concierge staterooms on the Disney Dream and Disney Fantasy have wooden doors, not magnetic ones — magnets simply won't stick. If you're in one of those rooms, a fish extender is your best option instead.

— Sizing it right

Door number plate sizes

Every stateroom door has a built-in number plate you can decorate around. On the Magic, Wonder, Dream, and Fantasy, it's a circle roughly 7.5 inches in diameter. On the newer ships — Wish, Treasure, and Destiny — it's a curved-edge rectangle, roughly 4.5 by 7 inches. Many guests build their door magnet design around this shape, having a character "peek out" from behind the number plate.

— Getting started

Ideas within the new rules

  • A large central character magnet sized to your door, with smaller magnets around it
  • A "countdown" or cruise-number magnet if this isn't your first sailing
  • Birthday, anniversary, or first-cruise celebration signage — all magnetic
  • A family name banner across the top of the door
  • Pack decorations in your carry-on, not checked luggage, so you can decorate as soon as you board — before checked bags typically arrive
