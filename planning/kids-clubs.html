Youth clubs

Kids clubs by age — plus the parent FAQ.

Oceaneer, Edge, Vibe, and the nursery: who goes where, how check-in works, open houses, port days, and the questions parents ask most.

Last updated: August 17, 2026

— Security

Check-in, self check-out, and who can pick kids up

  • Register first. Complete youth activities registration during online check-in when available, then finish on embarkation day at the Oceaneer desk (sometimes available in the terminal). Kids get an Oceaneer Band — or use a purchased DisneyBand+ on ships that support it. You’ll set a secret password and list who is allowed to pick the child up.
  • Checking in. Disney’s official rule: all registered children can check themselves into Oceaneer Club or Lab. Counselors still scan the band and keep the space in secured mode during regular programming.
  • Leaving / self check-out age. Children 8 and older may be given self-check privileges at registration. With that flag on, they can leave Oceaneer without an authorized adult. Kids under 8 must be signed out by an authorized adult — no exceptions for “just going back to the room.”
  • Who can take them out of the club. Only adults you authorize at registration. Pickup typically requires the adult’s Key to the World (or DisneyBand+) and the secret password. Counselors will not release a child to someone who isn’t on the authorized list, even if they’re “family” sailing in another cabin.
  • Who can take them off the ship. Leaving the vessel is a security/immigration process separate from club pickup. In practice, only adults on the child’s reservation (or otherwise cleared with Guest Services) can escort a minor ashore. If grandparents, a nanny, or friends in another stateroom need shore privileges with your kids, sort that with Guest Services early — don’t wait until a port morning at the gangway.
  • Edge and Vibe. Tweens and teens register once, then check themselves in and out. Rules are much looser than Oceaneer; counselors still staff the spaces.
  • Nursery pickup. Only adults listed at nursery check-in can retrieve a child; counselors expect ID / Key to the World and may review feeding or nap notes with you at drop-off. Nursery kids do not get the same self-check option as Oceaneers.
— Schedule

Hours (sea days, port days, nursery)

  • Oceaneer Club / Lab: Often roughly 9:00 a.m. to midnight (sometimes later) on sea days. Embarkation day usually opens later, after you’ve boarded and registered. Port days run shorter, with fewer activities — always trust that day’s Navigator schedule over a prior cruise.
  • Edge / Vibe: Skew afternoon and evening more than all-day Oceaneer hours.
  • Nursery: Commonly about 9:00 a.m. to 11:00 p.m., with a one-hour minimum booking. Hours vary by ship and itinerary; some port days extend coverage for parents on excursions. Disney’s own FAQ: nursery babysitting is available in port, subject to reservation and capacity.
  • How to confirm: Open the Disney Cruise Line Navigator app → youth activities / nursery schedule for your sailing. Printed Personal Navigators are gone; the app is the source of truth.
— Family time in the clubs

What are open houses?

Open House is when Oceaneer Club, Lab, Edge, and/or Vibe switch out of secured drop-off mode so guests of any age can walk in together — parents, grandparents, siblings under 3, teens, everyone. No check-in band required for that window.

  • Embarkation afternoon almost always includes open-house time so families can tour the rooms, meet counselors, and let shy kids warm up before a first drop-off.
  • More open-house blocks usually appear throughout the cruise; Club and Lab often alternate — one space open for family play while the other stays in secured programming so drop-off childcare continues.
  • Photos are allowed during Open House and generally not allowed during secured programming (including by ship photographers). If you want pictures of your child in the themed rooms, plan an Open House visit.
  • Open House is not babysitting. If you need counselors to take custody of your child, wait for secured hours (or use the space that is still secured while the other is Open House).
— Characters

Do characters go to the clubs?

Yes — on many sailings, characters visit Oceaneer spaces (and sometimes the nursery) for short appearances, crafts, or meet-and-greets. Timing posts in Navigator and is sailing-specific.

  • Some character moments happen during Open House (easier for parents to photograph); others happen during secured programming.
  • Wish-class rooms (Marvel Super Hero Academy, Star Wars: Cargo Bay, and similar) lean hard into character/IP play even when a costumed character isn’t in the room.
  • Character visits in club are a bonus, not a guarantee every day — don’t skip atrium or deck meets if a specific character is a must-see.
— Ashore vs onboard

Can kids stay on the ship while you go on port adventures?

Yes, within club hours and your comfort level — Disney does not require parents to stay onboard just because kids are in youth activities.

  • Nursery (under 3): Official Disney answer — group babysitting is available while the ship is in port, with an hourly fee, reservation, and capacity limits. Book early; peak excursion mornings sell out.
  • Oceaneer (3–10): Kids can remain checked into secured programming while adults go ashore, as long as the club is open. Tell youth activities management you’re leaving the ship, share how to reach you (and which Port Adventure you’re on, if any), and make sure an authorized adult (or a self-check child 8+) can handle pickup if plans change.
  • Edge / Vibe: Same idea — clubs usually stay open on port days with shorter hours. Tweens/teens with more independence may bounce between club, pool, and stateroom; set clear expectations before you leave.
  • Practical caution: Club hours can end before your excursion returns. Have a backup authorized adult onboard, or avoid booking the longest tours with kids who can’t self-check. All-aboard is absolute — counselors cannot hold the ship.
  • Private islands: Castaway Cay and Lookout Cay have their own kids areas (for example, Scuttle’s Cove / Sebastian’s Cove patterns). Ship nursery communication can be less reliable when the ship is far from shore — Destiny parents: see toddler tips.
— Staffing

Staff-to-child ratios

  • Oceaneer Club / Lab: Disney states youth programming follows Florida state childcare guidelines. They do not publish a single public “1 counselor per X kids” number for Oceaneer; ratios shift with age mix and how busy the space is (port mornings are often quieter than post-dinner evenings).
  • Nursery: Commonly reported (and longstanding DCL practice) as about 1:4 for younger infants and 1:6 for older toddlers — treat as a planning guide, not a contract. Capacity caps keep rooms from packing beyond what counselors can supervise.
  • Not one-on-one care. Clubs and nursery cannot provide dedicated 1:1 counselors or counselor-assisted medical care. Kids who need that level of support should contact Disney Cruise Line Special Services before sailing; see the accessibility guide.
— By ship class

How clubs differ ship to ship

Age bands, registration, passwords, and Open House rules are fleet-wide. What changes is theming, room layout, and a few ship-specific spaces.

  • Wish / Treasure / Destiny / Believe: Multi-room Oceaneer with Marvel, Star Wars, Mickey/Minnie, and Imagineering-style labs (exact names shift by ship). Destiny adds toddler play (Mickey & Minnie Captain’s Deck). Wish-class also has evening Hideaway hours for ~18–20. Destiny details: Destiny clubs. Believe venues are still TBA — use Wish-class ages from this page.
  • Dream / Fantasy: Classic Oceaneer Club + Lab split with popular themed rooms (Andy’s Room, Pixie Hollow, and similar era favorites depending on refurb status).
  • Magic / Wonder: Smaller ships, same age structure; Wonder hosts Bluey programming on select sailings. Clubs feel especially central because the ship is compact.
  • Adventure (Singapore): Youth clubs follow Disney’s age model inside Adventure’s themed lands (including Marvel-linked Oceaneer programming). Don’t assume Wish-class deck maps — verify room names and hours in Navigator.
  • Nursery location: Often Deck 5 on Magic / Wonder / Dream / Fantasy; Deck 2 near Oceaneer on Wish-class ships. Always confirm on your deck plan.
— Also asked a lot

More common questions

  • Is Oceaneer free? Yes. Nursery is the paid piece (typically around $9/hour for the first child and $8/hour for each additional child from the same family — confirm current rates when you book). Cancel nursery holds at least ~4 hours ahead or you may still be charged.
  • How many nursery hours can I reserve ahead? Families can usually lock a set advance block (often up to about 10 hours) in the Castaway Club activity window; more time can sometimes be added onboard if capacity allows. Windows: booking windows tool.
  • Must kids be potty trained? Yes for Oceaneer Club/Lab secured programming. Pull-ups don’t count. Nursery covers diapers. Not-yet-trained toddlers can still visit Oceaneer during Open House with a parent.
  • Can siblings stay together? Within Oceaneer, kids can often move between Club and Lab with counselor help. A 7-year-old and an 11-year-old cannot share Oceaneer secured programming — Edge is the tween space. Nursery is separate from Oceaneer.
  • Food and naps? Oceaneer may offer light snacks and later-evening rest mats for younger kids still checked in past bedtime. Nursery provides toddler snacks/drinks for kids eating solids; bring bottles, formula, baby food, diapers, wipes, a change of clothes, and a labeled comfort item. Under ~12 months, crib rules are stricter (pacifiers only is a common safety note).
  • How do counselors reach parents? Through the Navigator app (and ship systems tied to your stateroom / Key to the World). Old “Wave Phones” are no longer the main parent-paging method on current sailings — keep notifications on for the app.
  • Sick kids? Contagious illness usually means no club until cleared. When in doubt, Guest Services / medical can advise; don’t send a feverish child hoping counselors won’t notice.
  • Special needs? Programs are for children who are toilet-trained (Oceaneer), can participate in group ratios, and interact with peers. Modified support may be arranged through Special Services in advance — not as walk-up 1:1 care. More: accessibility.
  • Is there in-room babysitting? No. Nursery (under 3) or Oceaneer / Edge / Vibe (ages 3+) are the onboard options. Destiny toddler notes: Destiny toddler tips.
  • Disembarkation morning? Clubs are typically closed or very limited — don’t plan a last drop-off while you finish packing. See the disembarkation checklist.
— Parent tips

Registration and expectations

  • Register Oceaneer on embarkation day early — it’s one of the highest-ROI lines to stand in before sail-away.
  • Book nursery peak dinner and spa hours as soon as your Castaway Club activity window opens.
  • Tour during Open House, take photos then, and save secured hours for true drop-off time.
  • If you’re going ashore without the kids, tell youth activities (and nursery) your plan and return window.
  • Always trust Navigator over memory from a prior cruise — hours and character visits change by sailing.
