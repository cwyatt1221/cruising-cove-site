Should you book it yourself, or use an agent?

Same price either way — Disney pays the agent’s commission, not you. Here’s how to decide in two minutes.

— Start here

First, a few decisions

Before staterooms and dining times, there’s one question worth settling early: are you planning this yourself, or bringing in a travel agent? It’s free either way — so it really just comes down to how much of the planning you want to do.

— Quick take

Which path fits you?

Use an agent if

  • This is your first Disney cruise
  • You’re overwhelmed by staterooms, dining, or Port Adventures
  • You’re planning something high-stakes — honeymoon, reunion, milestone trip

Book it yourself if

  • You’ve cruised Disney before and know what you want
  • You already know your ship, date, and stateroom category
  • You enjoy the planning process and want full control of timing
— Side by side

The full comparison

Book it yourself Use a travel agent
Cost Same price — Disney doesn’t discount for booking direct Free — Disney pays the agent’s commission, not you
Best for You already know which ship, date, and stateroom you want First Disney cruise, or you’re overwhelmed by the options
Control Full control over timing — you book the moment your window opens Agent handles booking windows and paperwork for you
Research High — you’re on your own for staterooms, dining, Port Adventures Low — agent recommends based on your family
Post-booking None — Disney’s own support line, no personal advocate One person to call — price-drop monitoring, changes, day-of support
Good fit if You’ve cruised before, or you enjoy the planning process You want it handled, or it’s a high-stakes trip

“But is there a catch?”

No, really — it’s free. Disney pays travel agents a commission for bringing them business, the same way a hotel pays a booking site. Your price doesn’t change whether you book directly with Disney or through an agent.

What you get with an agent is a person who already knows the fine print — stateroom quirks, which Port Adventures sell out first, how to word a request for a connecting cabin — and who you can call when a question comes up mid-trip.

The trade-off isn’t price. It’s whether you’d rather do the research yourself or hand it to someone who does this daily.
