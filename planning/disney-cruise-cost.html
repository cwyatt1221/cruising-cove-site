— The honest number

What's actually included in the fare

Your base fare covers the stateroom, all rotational dining (main dining rooms and the buffet), entertainment, youth clubs, and most beach access at the private islands, including the BBQ lunch. That's genuinely a lot — more all-inclusive than most mainstream cruise lines. But gratuities, specialty dining, alcohol, Wi-Fi, spa services, and excursions are all separate, and none of them show up in the sticker price you book against.

— The real add-ons

Where the extra spending actually goes

Gratuities. Disney's recommended standard gratuity is $16.00 per person, per night, covering your stateroom host and dining team. Concierge staterooms and suites recommend $27.75 per person, per night. For a family of four on a 7-night sailing in a standard stateroom, that's roughly $448 in gratuities alone — easy to overlook if you're only budgeting the fare. These can be prepaid before sailing or settled onboard.

Specialty dining. Palo (or Palo Steakhouse on the newer ships) runs roughly $50–55 per person for brunch or dinner. Remy and Enchanté run higher, as fine-dining venues with a prix-fixe format. A gratuity is added separately to these bills, on top of the meal price.

Wi-Fi. Free basic connectivity covers iMessage, WhatsApp, and the Navigator app. Real internet access is tiered: roughly $16–18/day for social-media-only access, $24–28/day for browsing and email, with a premium tier priced higher still for full streaming speed.

Alcohol. Individual drinks run $10–15 plus an automatic 18% gratuity. You're allowed to bring aboard two bottles of wine or six beers per adult at embarkation and at each port, which is the main way experienced cruisers manage this cost.

Excursions. Disney's own Port Adventures range from roughly $50 to $300+ per person depending on the activity — a real budget swing depending on how many stops you book activities at.
