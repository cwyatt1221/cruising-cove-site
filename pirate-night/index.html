Onboard traditions

Pirate Night.

A themed dinner, a deck party, and fireworks at sea — Disney is the only cruise line in the world authorized to do it.

— Quick facts

— What it is

The evening, start to finish

Pirate Night is a themed evening featured on most Caribbean and Bahamas Disney sailings of three nights or longer — it's not offered on Alaska or Northern Europe itineraries due to local regulations. The evening includes a pirate-themed dinner menu, character meet-and-greets with Disney characters in pirate costume (including rarer appearances from Captain Hook and Mr. Smee), and builds to a deck party and fireworks show late in the evening.

On the newer Wish-class ships (Wish, Treasure, Destiny), the deck party is called the Pirates Rockin' Parlay Party — live music, special effects, and appearances from Captain Redd and Captain Jack Sparrow himself, set to the Pirates of the Caribbean soundtrack.

— Worth knowing

Fireworks at sea

Disney Cruise Line is the only cruise line in the world authorized to set off fireworks at sea — it's a genuine point of pride for the line and the highlight of the night. Fireworks launch from the starboard side (the right side facing forward), so that's where you want to be. The best viewing spots are upper decks 12 or 13, midship or toward the aft — avoid the port (left) side, where you'll see little to nothing.

— For younger kids

If you're worried the fireworks noise will overwhelm a young child, you can watch from the interior windows on decks 4 and 5, or from your stateroom balcony if it's on the starboard side.

— What to wear

Dress code

Dressing up is completely optional — you won't be the only one in regular clothes if you'd rather skip it. That said, most guests wear at least one accessory, often just the free red bandana provided in your stateroom. For a fuller look: flowy shirts, vests, sashes, and eye patches for adults; mini pirate hats, vests, or a themed t-shirt for kids. Comfortable shoes matter more than the costume itself — you'll be standing for the deck party and fireworks.

Costumes and accessories are available to buy onboard, but they run expensive. Packing your own from home (or a low-cost Etsy find) is the better move.

— Planning tips

Making the most of it

  • Check the Navigator app for your specific sailing's party time — it's usually around 10:15 or 10:30pm but varies
  • Head to your viewing spot early; the deck gets crowded fast once the party starts
  • There are typically two deck shows plus a Club Pirate dance party before the Pirates of the Caribbean film plays on FunnelVision
  • On the Disney Wish, watch for the atrium chandelier turning red to help set the mood
