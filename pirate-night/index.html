Onboard traditions

Pirate Night.

A themed dinner, a deck party, and fireworks at sea — a fleet signature few cruise lines match regularly.

— Quick facts

— What it is

The evening, start to finish

Pirate Night is a themed evening featured on most Caribbean and Bahamas Disney sailings of three nights or longer. Alaska and Northern Europe sailings typically run a Frozen deck party instead. The evening includes a pirate-themed dinner menu, character meet-and-greets with Disney characters in pirate costume (including rarer appearances from Captain Hook and Mr. Smee), and builds to a deck party — often with fireworks on Caribbean/Bahamas itineraries (not guaranteed everywhere; many Mediterranean Pirate Nights skip fireworks).

On the newer Wish-class ships (Wish, Treasure, Destiny), the deck party is called the Pirates Rockin' Parlay Party — live music, special effects, and appearances from Captain Redd and Captain Jack Sparrow himself, set to the Pirates of the Caribbean soundtrack.

— Worth knowing

Fireworks at sea

At-sea fireworks are a Disney Cruise Line signature on many Pirate Nights — especially Caribbean and Bahamas sailings — but they are not guaranteed on every itinerary. When fireworks fly, they usually launch from the starboard side (the right side facing forward). Best views are typically upper open decks midship or aft: decks 12–13 on Dream-/Wish-class, or the highest outdoor decks on Magic/Wonder. Port-side guests often still see the sky show; starboard is simply the launch side.

— For younger kids

If you're worried the fireworks noise will overwhelm a young child, you can watch from interior windows on lower decks, or from your stateroom balcony if it's on a side with a clear sky view.

— What to wear

Dress code

Dressing up is completely optional — you won't be the only one in regular clothes if you'd rather skip it. That said, most guests wear at least one accessory, often just the free red bandana provided in your stateroom. For a fuller look: flowy shirts, vests, sashes, and eye patches for adults; mini pirate hats, vests, or a themed t-shirt for kids. Comfortable shoes matter more than the costume itself — you'll be standing for the deck party and fireworks.

Costumes and accessories are available to buy onboard, but they run expensive. Packing your own from home (or a low-cost online find) is the better move.

— Planning tips

Making the most of it

  • Check the Navigator app for your specific sailing's party time — it's usually around 10:15 or 10:30pm but varies
  • Head to your viewing spot early; the deck gets crowded fast once the party starts
  • There are typically two deck shows plus a Club Pirate dance party before the Pirates of the Caribbean film plays on FunnelVision
  • On the Disney Wish, watch for the atrium chandelier turning red to help set the mood
  • Need costume ideas and what else to pack? See the Pirate Night packing section
