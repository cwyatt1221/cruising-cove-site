Disney Treasure

The Wish's sister ship, reworked around a theme of adventure and exploration — deep blues and greens, Aladdin and Lion King–themed staterooms, and the world's first Coco-themed dining experience.

— The ship

Overview

The Disney Treasure joined the fleet in December 2024 as the second Wish-class ship, sharing the Wish's deck plans and stateroom layout but reworked around an "Adventureland at sea" theme — deep blues, greens, and design influences pulled from Africa and Asia, in deliberate contrast to the Wish's bright fairy-tale palette. Captain Hook and Peter Pan appear at the stern; a bronze Aladdin greets guests in the Grand Hall.

She currently sails 7-night Eastern and Western Caribbean itineraries from Port Canaveral, Florida.

— Where you'll sleep

Staterooms

Staterooms are themed throughout to Encanto, Luca, Up, Aladdin, Pocahontas, and Finding Nemo, with Lion King theming reserved for the Concierge level and the ship's Royal Suites themed to Rajah and Bagheera. The Tomorrow Tower Suite, set inside the ship's forward funnel, is a two-story, 1,966-square-foot suite with private elevator access. Of the roughly 1,256 staterooms, about 70% include a verandah, and 451 connecting doors run throughout the ship — matching the Wish.

— Where you'll eat

Dining

Rotational dining includes 1923, Worlds of Marvel, and Plaza de Coco — the world's first theatrical dining experience themed to Pixar's Coco, with a modern take on traditional Mexican fare and live entertainment built into the meal. Adult-exclusive dining includes Palo (steakhouse-style brunch and dinner) and Enchanté (fine dining). Marceline Market serves as the casual buffet.

— Worth knowing

Signature features

  • Plaza de Coco — the first-ever theatrical dining experience themed to a specific Disney/Pixar film
  • Same hull and deck plan as the Wish, with an entirely different design theme
  • Tomorrow Tower Suite: a two-story suite built inside the ship's funnel
  • Jumbeaux's Sweets, themed to Zootopia, is exclusive to this ship
— Look closer

Easter eggs & hidden details

The Treasure's stern features Captain Hook and Peter Pan, mid-chase — fitting for a ship built around adventure and exploration. In the Grand Hall, a bronze Aladdin statue greets guests at the base of the staircase, continuing the adventure theme into the ship's most photographed space.

As the Wish's sister ship, the Treasure shares her deck plan and structural layout almost exactly — the fun is in spotting how differently each space has been re-themed, from Cinderella's fairy-tale world on the Wish to Aladdin, Pocahontas, and Up-inspired details throughout the Treasure.
