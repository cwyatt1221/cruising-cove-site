— The ship

Overview

The Disney Treasure joined the fleet in December 2024 as the second Wish-class ship, sharing the Wish's deck plans and stateroom layout but reworked around an "Adventureland at sea" theme — deep blues, greens, and design influences pulled from Africa and Asia, in deliberate contrast to the Wish's bright fairy-tale palette. Captain Hook and Peter Pan appear at the stern; a bronze Aladdin greets guests in the Grand Hall.

She currently sails 7-night Eastern and Western Caribbean itineraries from Port Canaveral, Florida.

— Where you'll sleep

Staterooms

Staterooms are themed throughout to Encanto, Luca, Up, Aladdin, Pocahontas, and Finding Nemo, with Lion King theming reserved for the Concierge level and the ship's Royal Suites themed to Rajah and Bagheera. The Tomorrow Tower Suite, set inside the ship's forward funnel, is a two-story, 1,966-square-foot suite with private elevator access. Of the roughly 1,256 staterooms, about 70% include a verandah, and 451 connecting doors run throughout the ship — matching the Wish.

— Where you'll eat · Wish-class

Dining on the Treasure

Same Wish-class system as the Wish template — rotational trio + adult specialty + casual — with Plaza de Coco instead of Arendelle. Seven-night Caribbean sailings mean you’ll repeat rooms and hit special menus (Pirate Night, Captain’s Gala, Día de los Muertos). System overview: Dining hub.

  • Adult specialty — Palo Steakhouse · Enchanté

— Included · dinner

Rotational restaurants

Early or late seating; servers rotate with you. On 7-night sailings you revisit rooms — Worlds of Marvel and Plaza de Coco each run distinct “second night” shows, so repeats are not carbon copies.

Rotational

1923

Walt & Roy Disney rooms — classic Hollywood glamour and animation heritage. Usually the calmest rotational night for conversation. Shared concept with Wish.

Rotational · show dinner

Plaza de Coco

Coco theatrical dining (Treasure exclusive) — mariachi energy, Miguel appearances, and a second-night Día de los Muertos celebration. Protect this if the film matters to your family.

Rotational · interactive

Worlds of Marvel

Avengers: Quantum Encounter plus Treasure’s Groot Remix celebration night — screen-driven fun; plan accordingly if you want a quiet date dinner that evening.

— Planning tip

On a week-long sailing you can fit one adult specialty night without skipping Plaza de Coco — book Palo/Enchanté after you know which rotational nights are show nights in Navigator.

— Book-first shows

Theatre seating is first-come for most sailings — arrive 15–20 minutes early for center/aisle seats on the “must-see” production for your ship (Wish: Little Mermaid; Dream/Fantasy: Beauty and the Beast or Frozen; Magic/Wonder: check Navigator). Pair show nights with your dining seating so you’re not racing from dessert.

— Hours & check-in

Oceaneer Club / Lab typically opens mid-morning through late evening (often ~9am–midnight on sea days; shorter on port days). Edge and Vibe skew afternoon/evening. Register Oceaneer on embarkation day; nursery is reservation + fee. Confirm exact hours in Navigator for your sailing.

— Character meets

Character greeting times post in the Navigator app once you’re onboard (and sometimes the night before). Popular meets — Mickey, princesses, Marvel / Pixar on themed ships — draw lines early on embarkation afternoon. Free character phone calls can be scheduled pre-cruise for many sailings.

— Worth knowing

Signature features

  • Same hull and deck plan as the Wish, with an entirely different design theme
  • Tomorrow Tower Suite: a two-story suite built inside the ship's funnel
  • Jumbeaux's Sweets, themed to Zootopia, is exclusive to this ship

— Look closer

Easter eggs & hidden details

The Treasure's stern features Captain Hook and Peter Pan, mid-chase — fitting for a ship built around adventure and exploration. In the Grand Hall, a bronze Aladdin statue greets guests at the base of the staircase, continuing the adventure theme into the ship's most photographed space.

As the Wish's sister ship, the Treasure shares her deck plan and structural layout almost exactly — the fun is in spotting how differently each space has been re-themed, from Cinderella's fairy-tale world on the Wish to Aladdin, Pocahontas, and Up-inspired details throughout the Treasure.
