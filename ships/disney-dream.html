— The ship

Overview

The Disney Dream debuted in January 2011 as the third ship in the fleet and the first of the Dream class, significantly larger than the Magic and Wonder at 1,250 staterooms and a 4,000-passenger capacity. She introduced the AquaDuck — the first water coaster ever installed on a cruise ship — a 765-foot tube ride with a four-deck drop that wraps around the ship's upper decks.

Built at Meyer Werft in Papenburg, Germany, the Dream styles herself in Art Deco, distinct from her sister ship the Fantasy's Art Nouveau look. A September–October 2024 dry dock added Ramone's Cantina, a new quick-service option on the pool deck, alongside interior and stateroom refreshes.

— Where you'll sleep

Staterooms

Inside staterooms on the Dream include a fleet first: virtual portholes, high-definition displays showing a live ocean view piped in from a camera mounted outside the ship. All four standard categories — Inside, Oceanview, Verandah, and Concierge — are available across the ship's cabin decks, with the usual split-bathroom layout throughout.

— Free onboard game

Midship Detective Agency

A free interactive mystery scavenger hunt unique to the Dream-class ships. Pick up a badge and clue cards in the atrium, then hold cards up to enchanted artwork around the ship as paintings and displays come alive with clues. Cases can include Stolen Puppies, Missing Masterpiece, and a Muppets adventure — plan 1–2 hours, ideally on a sea day.

— Worth knowing

Signature features

  • First DCL ship with the AquaDuck water coaster — still one of the most popular features fleet-wide
  • Two adult specialty restaurants (Palo and Remy) rather than one
  • Virtual portholes bring a live outside view to windowless inside staterooms
  • Shares almost her entire layout and venue list with sister ship the Fantasy
— Look closer

Easter eggs & hidden details

The Dream's stern character is Sorcerer Mickey, styled after his Fantasia appearance, depicted directing his enchanted brooms to finish painting the ship's name. In the atrium, the statue is Admiral Donald Duck.

Several decks hide small animated scenes worth detouring for: on Deck 5 midship starboard, a hidden panel comes to life with a crab scuttling across a map, a sea monster, and a miniature pirate-ship battle — and nearby, turning Captain Hook's ship wheel lets you steer an animated Jolly Roger around Neverland Lagoon. Deck 6 forward (by the elevators) hides three scenes from the 1938 cartoon Boat Builders, with Mickey, Donald, and Goofy each building a doomed boat. Deck 7 aft has an animated cel from the 1937 short Hawaiian Holiday.
