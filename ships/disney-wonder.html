The Magic's twin, with her own personality: Art Nouveau styling, the exclusive Tiana's Place restaurant, and the same intimate, classic-class feel.

— The ship

Overview

The Disney Wonder set sail August 15, 1999, as the fleet's second ship and near-identical twin to the Magic in structure and layout. Where the Magic leans Art Deco, the Wonder is styled Art Nouveau — the difference shows up in details throughout the ship rather than the overall floor plan, which the two ships share almost exactly.

A 2016 dry dock in Cadiz, Spain brought AquaLab (an interactive water play area) and Dory's Reef toddler splash zone to the upper decks, alongside Marvel-themed additions to the Oceaneer Club and an adults-only English pub. The ship's horn plays the first seven notes of "When You Wish Upon a Star," and her godmother is Tinker Bell.

— Where you'll sleep

Staterooms

Like the Magic, the Wonder offers Inside, Oceanview, Verandah, and Concierge staterooms across 6 decks of cabins, with the same split-bathroom layout found fleet-wide. Sixteen staterooms are modified for accessibility.

— Where you'll eat

Dining

Rotational dining moves guests through Triton's (the main dining room), Animator's Palate, and Tiana's Place — a New Orleans–themed restaurant exclusive to this ship, added in the 2016 refurbishment, known for its shrimp and grits and beignets. Casual dining runs through the buffet and poolside quick-service venues shared fleet-wide.

— Worth knowing

Signature features

  • Tiana's Place is exclusive to the Wonder — not found on any other DCL ship
  • Frozen, A Musical Spectacular is the ship's signature stage show
  • Feels noticeably more intimate than the larger Dream-, Wish-, and Treasure-class ships
  • Popular for Alaska sailings, where the smaller size suits the itinerary well
— Look closer

Easter eggs & hidden details

The Wonder's stern character is Donald Duck and his mischievous nephews — a playful contrast to the Magic's more formal Captain Mickey theming. Just outside Triton's in the atrium, look for a statue of Princess Ariel, a nod to the restaurant's underwater theming.

The ship's horn plays the first seven notes of "When You Wish Upon a Star" — listen for it at departure. The Wonder's godmother (every DCL ship is formally "christened" with one) is Tinker Bell.
