Disney Wish

The first Wish-class ship, built around a theme of enchantment — home to the AquaMouse, Disney's first branded attraction at sea, and a newer generation of staterooms shared with Treasure and Destiny.

— The ship

Overview

The Disney Wish set sail in July 2022 as the first ship of the Wish class, larger and newer than the Dream-class ships and themed around enchantment and classic Disney fairy tales. She introduced the AquaMouse — the first Disney-branded attraction ever built at sea — alongside theming pulled from Marvel, Star Wars, and Pixar throughout the ship. Sisters Treasure and Destiny are already sailing; Believe is next in the class.

At 1,254 staterooms and 15 decks (10 of them holding cabins), she's built for 4,000 guests and a crew of roughly 1,555. She currently sails 3- and 4-night Bahamas itineraries from Port Canaveral, with new European sailings planned to begin in summer 2027.

— Where you'll sleep

Staterooms

Ninety percent of the Wish's 1,254 staterooms offer an ocean view, and roughly 948 of those include a verandah. All standard categories are available — Inside, Oceanview, Verandah, and Concierge — with 451 connecting doors throughout the ship to accommodate larger families and groups, and the familiar split-bathroom layout in every room.

Dining on the Wish

Wish-class ships use the same dining system as the rest of the fleet (rotational + adult specialty + casual), but the restaurant names and experiences are new. Treasure and Destiny follow this same map with different theming. System overview: Dining hub.

  • Rotational trio — 1923 · Arendelle · Worlds of Marvel
  • Adult specialty — Palo Steakhouse · Enchanté
  • Casual & quick service
  • Shows, clubs & nightlife
  • Bluey & Bingo — select Port Canaveral sailings from June 2026
— Included · dinner

Rotational restaurants

You’re assigned early or late seating and rotate through three main dining rooms. Your servers move with you. On a 3-night sailing you typically hit each room once; on a 4-night sailing you repeat one room (often with a Pirate Night menu on the themed evening).

Rotational

1923

Walt Disney Animation Studios–inspired elegance — classic Hollywood glamour, often the “quietest” of the three for conversation. Strong pick if you want a traditional fine-casual dinner vibe.

Rotational · show dinner

Arendelle: A Frozen Dining Adventure

Immersive Frozen storytelling while you eat — louder, theatrical, and the night many families refuse to skip. Don’t book Palo over this if kids are counting on Anna & Elsa.

Rotational · interactive

Worlds of Marvel

Marvel mission energy with interactive table tech / storytelling beats. Fun for Marvel fans; expect more spectacle than a quiet date night. Full guide & guest reports →

— Planning tip

On short Bahamas sailings, protect Arendelle if Frozen matters to your group — then pick one adult specialty night (Palo or Enchanté), not both, so rotational storytelling doesn’t get squeezed out.

— Extra · adults 18+

Palo Steakhouse & Enchanté

Both sit on Deck 12 (entered via The Rose). Cover charges apply; book as soon as your window opens. Wish-class replaces classic Palo + Remy with Palo Steakhouse + Enchanté.

— Extra · families

Olaf’s Royal Picnic

Wish-exclusive paid character dining in Arendelle with Olaf, Anna, Elsa, and Kristoff — designed for kids about ages 3–12 (adults welcome as companions). Not offered on Treasure or Destiny. Book in your Castaway Club activity window; confirm your sailing in My Cruise Activities.

— After dark · Wish-class template

Entertainment on the Wish

Shows, youth clubs, and adult lounges share the same clock as dinner. System overview: Entertainment hub. Jump to shows, clubs, or nightlife.

— Walt Disney Theatre

Stage shows

The three-level Walt Disney Theatre runs the Wish’s Broadway-style slate. Showtimes are paired with dining seatings (early dinner → later show, late dinner → earlier show). Confirm exact times in Navigator once onboard.

Opening night energy

Disney Seas the Adventure

Embarkation-day (or early-sailing) original — Captain Minnie, Goofy, and cameo-heavy storytelling that introduces the ship’s theatrical style.

Wish exclusive

Disney’s The Little Mermaid

Created for the Wish — immersive undersea staging, projections, and a reimagined musical telling. Often the “don’t miss” for first-timers on this ship.

Classic spectacular

Disney’s Aladdin – A Musical Spectacular

High-energy Genie-forward production with sets, effects, and aisle moments. Arrive 15–20 minutes early for better seats.

  • Runtimes are roughly an hour — check Navigator if toddlers need an early exit plan.
  • Some sailings replay productions on stateroom TV; ask Guest Services if nap timing conflicts.
— Book-first shows

Theatre seating is first-come for most sailings — arrive 15–20 minutes early for center/aisle seats on the “must-see” production for your ship (Wish: Little Mermaid; Dream/Fantasy: Beauty and the Beast or Frozen; Magic/Wonder: check Navigator). Pair show nights with your dining seating so you’re not racing from dessert.

— Hours & check-in

Oceaneer Club / Lab typically opens mid-morning through late evening (often ~9am–midnight on sea days; shorter on port days). Edge and Vibe skew afternoon/evening. Register Oceaneer on embarkation day; nursery is reservation + fee. Confirm exact hours in Navigator for your sailing.

— Character meets

Character greeting times post in the Navigator app once you’re onboard (and sometimes the night before). Popular meets — Mickey, princesses, Marvel / Pixar on themed ships — draw lines early on embarkation afternoon. Free character phone calls can be scheduled pre-cruise for many sailings.

— Select sailings · families

Bluey & Bingo onboard

Bluey and Bingo join select Port Canaveral Wish sailings starting June 2026. Shorter 3–4 night Bahamian trips mean packing carefully around dining and shows — open the Disney Navigator app as soon as you board and mark every Bluey block. Confirm your dates include the programming before you book (or ask your travel advisor).

Expect morning Wakey Wakey with Bluey and Bingo (Dance Mode!, dancing, and games), character meet-and-greets with photo opportunities, the Who’s Behind the Curtain? family game show (yes-or-no questions until the big reveal, then a dance-party celebration), Bluey’s Favourite Games featuring Keepy Uppy and Ground is Lava, a scavenger hunt and Bluey-themed crafts, and evening Bluey and Bingo’s Pyjama Party (also called the Pyjama Bash) with freeze dancing, interactive games, and a special character appearance. Offerings can vary by sailing length and itinerary.

— Facts for Wish families
  • Who: Bluey and Bingo — the Blue Heeler sisters from the BBC Studios / Ludo Studio series.
  • Wish timeline: Select Port Canaveral sailings starting June 2026 (after Dream’s Fort Lauderdale launch in February).
  • Why Wish: Newer Wish-class design (AquaMouse, Grand Hall, Arendelle) plus Bluey — a strong pick if little ones want immersive ship spectacle and Heeler sisters on the same short trip.
  • Short-sailing tip: On 3-nighters, prioritize Wakey Wakey and at least one meet; book a 4-night if you want a better shot at the full slate without rushing.
  • Cost: Included with the cruise — no separate ticket — but only on sailings that list the programming.
  • European sailings: Officially excluded from Bluey programming fleet-wide (Wish’s first Europe season is planned for summer 2027 and would not include this offering under current rules).
  • Ages: Built for younger kids (roughly ages 2–8) and their families; adults are welcome at the parties too.
  • Timing tip: Embarkation-day meet-and-greets (often early afternoon) are frequently the least crowded chance for photos.
  • Also sailing in 2026: Dream (Fort Lauderdale since Feb 2026) and returning Wonder (San Diego from October 2026) on select sailings.

— Look closer

Easter eggs & hidden details

The Wish is genuinely dense with detail — independent counts have put it past 200 hidden references. A few of the best:

  • Rapunzel and Pascal sit on the stern, putting finishing touches on the ship's signage — Rapunzel is the first Disney princess ever placed on a DCL ship's stern.
  • The Grand Hall's chandelier is shaped to resemble Cinderella's dress mid-transformation, with four stars around it representing the Magic, Wonder, Dream, and Fantasy, and a brighter star on top representing the Wish herself. A glass slipper sits at the base of the grand staircase.
  • Look closely at the Cinderella statue in the Grand Hall: Lucifer the cat is stalking Jaq and Gus, who are hiding under her dress — a tiny nod to the film's climax.
  • An animated mural near the 1923 restaurants tells scenes from Mickey and the Beanstalk, including Willie the Giant and the Disney Wish herself sailing by inside the story.
  • The wayfinding icons embedded in the carpet change by area — a clock tower, glass slipper, pumpkin, or coach near Cinderella-themed spaces; a castle, owl, shell, or sun icon (from Tangled) elsewhere.
  • From the Observation Deck, look down at the Senses Spa awnings — from the right angle, they form a giant hidden Mickey.
