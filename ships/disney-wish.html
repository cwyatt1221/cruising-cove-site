— The ship

Overview

The Disney Wish set sail in July 2022 as the first ship of the new Wish class, larger and newer than the Dream-class ships and themed around enchantment and classic Disney fairy tales. She introduced the AquaMouse — the first Disney-branded attraction ever built at sea — alongside theming pulled from Marvel, Star Wars, and Pixar throughout the ship.

At 1,254 staterooms and 15 decks (10 of them holding cabins), she's built for 4,000 guests and a crew of roughly 1,555. She currently sails 3- and 4-night Bahamas itineraries from Port Canaveral, with new European sailings planned to begin in summer 2027.

— Where you'll sleep

Staterooms

Ninety percent of the Wish's 1,254 staterooms offer an ocean view, and roughly 948 of those include a verandah. All standard categories are available — Inside, Oceanview, Verandah, and Concierge — with 451 connecting doors throughout the ship to accommodate larger families and groups, and the familiar split-bathroom layout in every room.

— Where you'll eat · Wish-class template

Dining on the Wish

Wish-class ships use the same dining system as the rest of the fleet (rotational + adult specialty + casual), but the restaurant names and experiences are new. Treasure and Destiny follow this same map with different theming. System overview: Dining hub.

  • Rotational trio — 1923 · Arendelle · Worlds of Marvel
  • Adult specialty — Palo Steakhouse · Enchanté
  • Casual & quick service
  • Shows, clubs & nightlife
— Included · dinner

Rotational restaurants

You’re assigned early or late seating and rotate through three main dining rooms. Your servers move with you. On a 3-night sailing you typically hit each room once; on a 4-night sailing you repeat one room (often with a Pirate Night menu on the themed evening).

Rotational

1923

Walt Disney Animation Studios–inspired elegance — classic Hollywood glamour, often the “quietest” of the three for conversation. Strong pick if you want a traditional fine-casual dinner vibe.

Rotational · show dinner

Arendelle: A Frozen Dining Adventure

Immersive Frozen storytelling while you eat — louder, theatrical, and the night many families refuse to skip. Don’t book Palo over this if kids are counting on Anna & Elsa.

Rotational · interactive

Worlds of Marvel

Marvel mission energy with interactive table tech / storytelling beats. Fun for Marvel fans; expect more spectacle than a quiet date night.

— Planning tip

On short Bahamas sailings, protect Arendelle if Frozen matters to your group — then pick one adult specialty night (Palo or Enchanté), not both, so rotational storytelling doesn’t get squeezed out.

— Extra · adults 18+

Palo Steakhouse & Enchanté

Both sit on Deck 12 (entered via The Rose). Cover charges apply; book as soon as your window opens. Wish-class replaces classic Palo + Remy with Palo Steakhouse + Enchanté.

— After dark · Wish-class template

Entertainment on the Wish

Shows, youth clubs, and adult lounges share the same clock as dinner. System overview: Entertainment hub. Jump to shows, clubs, or nightlife.

— Walt Disney Theatre

Stage shows

The three-level Walt Disney Theatre runs the Wish’s Broadway-style slate. Showtimes are paired with dining seatings (early dinner → later show, late dinner → earlier show). Confirm exact times in Navigator once onboard.

Opening night energy

Disney Seas the Adventure

Embarkation-day (or early-sailing) original — Captain Minnie, Goofy, and cameo-heavy storytelling that introduces the ship’s theatrical style.

Wish exclusive

Disney’s The Little Mermaid

Created for the Wish — immersive undersea staging, projections, and a reimagined musical telling. Often the “don’t miss” for first-timers on this ship.

Classic spectacular

Disney’s Aladdin – A Musical Spectacular

High-energy Genie-forward production with sets, effects, and aisle moments. Arrive 15–20 minutes early for better seats.

  • Runtimes are roughly an hour — check Navigator if toddlers need an early exit plan.
  • Some sailings replay productions on stateroom TV; ask Guest Services if nap timing conflicts.

— Look closer

Easter eggs & hidden details

The Wish is genuinely dense with detail — independent counts have put it past 200 hidden references. A few of the best:

  • Rapunzel and Pascal sit on the stern, putting finishing touches on the ship's signage — Rapunzel is the first Disney princess ever placed on a DCL ship's stern.
  • The Grand Hall's chandelier is shaped to resemble Cinderella's dress mid-transformation, with four stars around it representing the Magic, Wonder, Dream, and Fantasy, and a brighter star on top representing the Wish herself. A glass slipper sits at the base of the grand staircase.
  • Look closely at the Cinderella statue in the Grand Hall: Lucifer the cat is stalking Jaq and Gus, who are hiding under her dress — a tiny nod to the film's climax.
  • An animated mural near the 1923 restaurants tells scenes from Mickey and the Beanstalk, including Willie the Giant and the Disney Wish herself sailing by inside the story.
  • The wayfinding icons embedded in the carpet change by area — a clock tower, glass slipper, pumpkin, or coach near Cinderella-themed spaces; a castle, owl, shell, or sun icon (from Tangled) elsewhere.
  • From the Observation Deck, look down at the Senses Spa awnings — from the right angle, they form a giant hidden Mickey.
